# Execution Readiness

Use this instruction-only workflow for a substantial module, refactor, or
milestone handoff intended for sustained execution with reduced human decisions.
Assess whether the **entire delegated scope** can reach its agreed endpoint under
existing authority. A runnable first task and complete-looking documents do not
establish that conclusion. Automatic discovery and native question rendering
depend on the host; this workflow is not a background hook or watcher.

## 1. Enter Through the Current Task or a Manual Request

- **Automatic:** Finish the current task's requested development preparation
  first, then check readiness when the handoff appears complete and starting
  execution remains an open decision. Do not inspect unrelated projects or
  intercept every message, ordinary edits, summaries, or status questions.
- **Manual:** A request to check whether a scope can enter execution authorizes
  assessment. Report the eligible next phase, evidence and gaps; do not infer
  permission to create planning artifacts or implement. A start choice may follow
  a passing assessment if the decision is open; explicit check-only or
  planning/documentation-only scope suppresses it.
- **Preparation requested:** Complete or repair source documents within existing
  authorization, then recheck. A missing normative decision goes to its owner;
  a factual gap calls for permitted evidence gathering, not an invented
  requirement or an unnecessary human decision.
- **Execution authorized:** Reuse valid readiness evidence and continue. For
  entry into this sustained workflow, resolve material gaps before dependent
  work without re-asking for authorization already granted. Ordinary authorized
  implementation does not acquire a new universal readiness gate.

Declining, deferring, or choosing to view the assessment suppresses another
unsolicited offer for the same scope. An explicit request to reconsider reopens
the check. A resume or incidental document edit does not clear that choice.

## 2. Separate Preparation Entry from Execution Admission

For milestone planning, first establish the existing [three document inputs](milestone-execution.md#0-verify-the-three-inputs-before-entry):
authority, a substantive development document, and an explicit roadmap. These
permit deriving the cards and queue; do not require those derived artifacts
before allowing their authorized preparation.

Before sustained execution, require an accepted scope and decisions, a usable
development plan, and executable development documentation. Use an existing
queue and cards, including sufficient inline equivalents. For a general module
or refactor, do not manufacture a roadmap, extra files, five separate agents, or
milestone-specific model requirements. Conversely, an explicitly requested
milestone workflow retains its own entry and role/model policy.

A **milestone is a delivery acceptance node**: named deliverables, observable
criteria, dependencies, evidence, and an acceptance owner or established
mechanism. Discussion/planning stages are preparation unless their documents
are themselves agreed deliverables. Task completion alone does not pass a
milestone. A Goal can cover several nodes; the queue owns business progress and
evidence, while the host separately owns Goal lifecycle and budget.

## 3. Assess Feasibility Across the Delegated Scope

Read the execution entry, authority/decisions, plan, canonical queue and relevant
cards. Compare them with actual code, contracts, dependencies, environment/access
and verification entrypoints. Reuse current evidence and inspect changes;
distinguish observations, unverified claims and open decisions. Existing baseline
failures and not-yet-implemented feature tests are not by themselves proof of
infeasibility; identify their actual effect on delivery.

| Question | Sufficient evidence |
|---|---|
| What is being delegated? | Explicit outcome, inclusions/exclusions and endpoint; governing authority and acceptance rules are current and consistent. |
| Are consequential decisions settled? | Product rules, data handling, ownership/contracts and material tradeoffs along the critical path are accepted or have an explicitly agreed human checkpoint. They are not left as unbounded implementation discretion. |
| Can the delivery path work? | A coherent plan covers the whole scope; critical dependencies and technical assumptions have supporting evidence. Near-term tasks are actionable; later detail may stay open when delegated technical choices and activation conditions are clear. |
| Can the agent act and verify? | Required access, environment, fixtures, verification methods and role-appropriate effective configurations are established. A probe proves only its measured property; mocks do not prove real-service acceptance. |
| Can routine failures be handled? | The executor owns ordinary diagnosis and repair within scope; permitted recovery, stop conditions, integration responsibility and review/acceptance ownership are clear. |
| Where is human participation still needed? | Name remaining decision/acceptance points, their trigger, owner and effect on dependent work. Do not invent a participation percentage or imply those checkpoints have passed. |

An explicit human acceptance point can coexist with feasible autonomous work up
to that point. It must be visible in the agreed delivery path; missing acceptance
evidence still prevents milestone/Goal completion. An unresolved critical product
choice halfway through the scope prevents a blanket readiness claim, even if
T1 is READY. Report independently feasible work separately; do not silently
shrink the user's goal or invent a reduced execution path.

For consequential handoffs, use an independent fresh-context review when
available and within authorization. Supply the raw handoff and actual evidence.
Walk normal completion, an ordinary failure and a material scope/contract
deviation: can the reviewer identify next actions, decision owners, checks and
stopping points without filling gaps from unstated intent? Reuse an adequate
existing review; do not add one to every trivial task. An unavailable
project-required review remains a gap; otherwise disclose the verification limit.

Return one concise assessment in the existing execution entry or conversation:
the scope and endpoint; whether sustained execution is feasible; supporting
evidence; delegated decisions; expected human checkpoints; unresolved gaps and
owners; and the exact next action. These are findings, not new queue states.
Do not create a document hierarchy or a capability-adoption assessment merely
because this technical readiness check is requested.

If insufficient, complete independent authorized preparation and explain what
would resolve the gap. Do not offer immediate full-scope execution or label an
unauthorized task READY. For a passing assessment with an open start decision or applicable execution
authorization, read [Execution Handoff](execution-handoff.md) for the scoped
choice, Goal handling and ongoing readiness/acceptance rules before dispatch.
Explicit check-only or planning-only work ends with the assessment; do not load
that execution reference or inspect Goal state just to report readiness.
